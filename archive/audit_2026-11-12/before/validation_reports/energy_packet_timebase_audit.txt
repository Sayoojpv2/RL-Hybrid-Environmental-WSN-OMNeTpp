Questionable assumptions and sensitivities (per node, 500 h)
  - Water counts the DO probe (99.0 J) although DO is NOT in the payload (KSPCB DO preprocessing invalid): 304.8 J with DO, 205.8 J without. EZO-class currents and the
    1 s on-time are ASSUMPTIONS (datasheets not readable). Each extra second of on-time per pH/EC/DO reading adds 99 J per board.
  - Standby/sleep currents are NOT in the ledger (sensors assumed power-gated between samples). Every 1 mA left on at 3.3 V costs 5940 J per 500 h, so an un-gated interface board would dominate water and soil.
  - PMS5003: 15 J per wake-up = 5.0 V x 100 mA x 30 s, all VERIFIED values (manual v2.3), but 100 mA is the MAXIMUM
    working current, so 30.0 kJ is an upper bound. If the sensor sleeps instead of being power-cut, the VERIFIED
    standby current (<= 200 uA) adds 1740 J. Running it continuously would cost 900 kJ (30x).
  - Air gases: 4 electrochemical sensors biased continuously (they cannot be duty-cycled quickly); the 1 mA ISB
    supply current is an ASSUMPTION: 23760 J = 44 % of the air total.
  - Effluent: the UV-Vis COD/BOD probe (12 V x 250 mA x 20 s, ASSUMPTION) is 120000 J = 99.3 % of 120903 J. Such multi-watt
    analysers are normally mains- or solar-powered; without it the node needs 903 J.
  - Soil: probe (5 V x 10 mA x 150 ms) and pH/NPK readings are ASSUMPTIONS; low-cost RS485 "NPK" probes infer
    nutrients indirectly and their accuracy is unverified.
  - Units: every term is V x mA x ms = uJ (generator) = V x A x s = J (this audit); both agree (checks above).
  - All four profiles exceed the 2 J radio battery (soil 14x, water 152x, air 26,910x, effluent 60,451x), so
    sensing is kept in a SEPARATE ledger. The runs measure communication-network lifetime, not node battery life.

TIME BASE
======================================================================================================================
  1 round = 1 h of environmental time for sampling, reporting and the sensing ledger. 500 rounds = 500 h = 20.8 days.
  OMNeT++ reserves 100 s of simulated time per round (scheduling only; the network work of a round ends far earlier);
  path delay is a physical per-report time (k/Rbit + Tproc + d/c per hop), independent of the round slot.
  water_quality        4 samples/round = every 15 min; periodic report every 2 h; alarm report at most every 1 h while an alarm is active
  industrial_effluent  4 samples/round = every 15 min; periodic report every 1 h; alarm report at most every 1 h while an alarm is active
  air_quality          4 samples/round = every 15 min; periodic report every 1 h; alarm report at most every 1 h while an alarm is active
  soil_monitoring      2 samples/round = every 30 min; periodic report every 4 h; alarm report at most every 1 h while an alarm is active; chemistry every 24 rounds = daily
  Water VALUES keep the validated KSPCB schedule: 212 sampling dates (2025-01-01 .. 2025-07-31) spread over
    1000 rounds, i.e. one KSPCB date every 4.735 rounds; rounds 1-500 replay the dates up to ~date index 105 (mid-April 2025).
    The value stream is therefore time-compressed 5.07x relative to the 1-h round (daily grab samples,
    sample-and-hold). This affects which values / alarms appear, never energy or the schedule. If the round were taken
    as the KSPCB slot (~5.07 h) instead, water sensing would rise to 1545 J: this inconsistency is removed by fixing 1 round = 1 h.
  Air: 500 consecutive UCI hours (2016-12-01 00:00 + 0..499 h). Effluent, soil: synthetic hourly series T+0..499 h.

PACKET SIZE AUDIT
======================================================================================================================
  Application header 10 B: node_id 2 B, sequence 2 B, round 2 B, profile_id 1 B, flags 1 B (alarm, priority, report type), field-presence bitmap 2 B.
  Each field: 16-bit scaled integer (value / scale); absent fields are cleared in the presence bitmap and not sent.
  MAC/PHY overhead 17 B: IEEE 802.15.4: PHY 6 B (preamble 4, SFD 1, PHR 1) + MAC 11 B (FCF 2, seq 1, dst PAN 2, dst 2, src 2, FCS 2) with 16-bit addresses and PAN-ID compression.
  IEEE 802.15.4 limit: PSDU <= 127 B, i.e. <= 116 B of MAC payload with this 11 B MAC header + FCS.

  Mode 1 / Mode 2T: the reference 4000-bit packet (500 B) of the MATLAB first-order radio model. It is an abstraction:
    500 B exceeds the 127 B 802.15.4 PSDU (it would need 5 frames). The 18-28 B measurement payload is carried inside it.
  Aggregation: the cluster head concatenates reports without compression and is charged per report (report-preserving
    energy: m reports = m x packetSize on TX and RX, plus E_DA x packetSize x m). Aggregation never changes the bits per
    report. Real 802.15.4 concatenation could share the 17 B overhead (up to the reports/127B column), which the model
    does not credit, so Mode 2F radio energy is an upper bound for the given traffic.
  Mode 2F charges every report its own profile frame (scenarioFrameBits: water 280, effluent 360, air 360, soil 264,
    328 on chemistry rounds; mixed nodes keep their own profile). One-packet thresholds use the frame mean report bits;
    the minimum-energy rule uses packetSize = the smallest frame of the scenario. Missing fields do not shrink M2F frames.
    Side effects of a smaller packetSize inside the unchanged model (documented, not hidden):
    - TX of one report over 50 m: 0.300 mJ at 4000 bit vs 0.0210-0.0270 mJ; the reward energy term
      min(1, E/1 mJ) shrinks by the same 11-14x factor, so the learned trade-off shifts towards link quality/progress.
    - minimum-operation rule E < E_elec x packetSize: 200 uJ at 4000 bit vs 14-18 uJ.
    - hop delay k/Rbit: 16 ms at 4000 bit vs 1.1-1.4 ms; Tproc = 5 ms then dominates.
    Mode 2T keeps all three exactly as in Mode 1; compare M2T with Mode 1 for the schedule effect and M2F with M2T for
    the frame-size effect.
